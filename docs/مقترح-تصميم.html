Why gold, not blue

Blue is what every top-up store already uses — it is the safe, invisible choice. Saffron gold is the colour of the thing being sold: coins, credit, UC. On an indigo ground it glows the way a lit stall does after dark, and it is legible on both light and dark screens.

#0B0A1A #15132E #FFB020 #FF4D6D #35E0AE #F6F1E7

The voucher, torn

A top-up is a voucher, so each package card is cut like one — notched at the sides with a dashed tear line across the middle. It is a shape nobody else in this market uses, it survives having no product photography, and it makes the price feel like something you hold.

Hex tokens for sections

Section icons sit inside a hexagon — the facet of a coin. It keeps the grid tight at three across, reads clearly at small sizes, and gives the catalogue a rhythm that a plain rounded square cannot.

Numbers get their own voice

Prices, quantities, order codes and player IDs are set in a monospaced face with tabular figures, so columns line up and a receipt reads like a receipt. Everything else uses the interface face. Two voices, each doing one job.
